Welcome to my research blog, where I share detailed insights into my work in particle physics, machine learning applications, and experimental discoveries at CERN. These posts provide deeper context and personal reflections on the research projects that drive my scientific curiosity.

## Recent Posts

### Machine Learning and Computational Physics

**[Teaching Machines to Reconstruct the Invisible: ν-Flows and ν²-Flows](/blog/nu-flows/)**
*January 11, 2024*

How we used normalising flows to reconstruct neutrinos in top-quark pair events – pushing machine learning in high-energy physics from classification to precise inference. This post explores the technical challenges of neutrino reconstruction and how conditional normalizing flows provide a revolutionary solution.

**[Graph Networks Meet Particle Physics: The Topographs Project](/blog/topographs/)**
*March 15, 2024*

Exploring how graph neural networks can naturally represent the complex relationships between particles in high-energy collisions, moving beyond traditional image-based approaches to capture the true structure of particle physics events.

### Precision Measurements and New Physics

**[Charmed Precision: Uncovering Rare Top-Quark Final States](/blog/ttcharm/)**
*September 26, 2024*

ATLAS has delivered its first dedicated measurement of top-quark pairs produced with additional charm jets — a challenging signature that I had the privilege to lead from start to finish. This post details the experimental challenges and theoretical implications of this groundbreaking measurement.

**[Beyond the Standard Model: Searching for Long-Lived Particles](/blog/toponium/)**
*August 8, 2024*

The search for new physics often requires looking beyond traditional signatures. This post explores our innovative approaches to detecting long-lived particles that decay to muons, using novel triggers and large-radius tracking techniques.

**[Photons and Top Quarks: Probing Electroweak Interactions](/blog/ttgamma/)**
*July 22, 2024*

When top quarks are produced with photons, we gain unique insights into electroweak interactions and potential new physics. This post discusses our precision measurement of tt̄γ production and its implications for understanding fundamental interactions.
