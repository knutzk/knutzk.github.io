I'm an experimental particle physicist exploring the fundamental nature of matter and energy through precision measurements and modern analysis techniques at the Large Hadron Collider.


## My Journey in Physics

I began my academic path at the University of Göttingen, where I developed a lasting interest in experimental research. What started as curiosity about the building blocks of matter soon became a career working with the most energetic collisions we can create.

My Ph.D. research, under the supervision of Prof. Arnulf Quadt, focused on rare processes involving top quarks and photons. This work required developing new analysis methods and earned *summa cum laude* honors, supported by a doctoral scholarship from the German Academic Scholarship Foundation.

In 2021, I moved to Geneva as a **Feodor Lynen Fellow** of the Humboldt Foundation to explore machine learning applications in collider physics. This shift allowed me to work on anomaly detection techniques and broadened my perspective on how AI and particle physics intersect.

Today, I'm based at CERN as a Postdoctoral Fellow with Harvard University, working on top-quark physics, searches for new phenomena, and machine learning–based event reconstruction.


## Collaboration and Outreach

My research happens within the ATLAS experiment at CERN, where I've taken on leadership roles that range from coordinating analysis efforts to ensuring data quality during detector operations. These experiences shaped how I think about collaboration — as both a scientific and organizational challenge.

Outside of analysis work, I enjoy guiding visitors through CERN, sharing what we do with students and the public. I also teach interdisciplinary workshops on machine learning and contribute to peer review and editorial work within the physics community.
